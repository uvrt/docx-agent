# Annual Water Quality Report 2025

Prepared by the Water Quality team, March 2026.

## Executive summary

In 2025 the Authority supplied drinking water to **412,000 customers** from six treatment works. Water met the regulatory standard in *99.96%* of all compliance samples, up from 99.94% in 2024.

Three points stand out:

- Microbiological compliance was the best on record.
- Two lead exceedances were traced to customer-side pipework.
- Nitrate levels at Eastmoor rose for the third year running.

## Monitoring programme

### Sampling sites

We sampled at 6 treatment works, 41 service reservoirs and 230 randomly selected customer taps. The sampling plan follows the national [drinking water guidance](https://example.org/dw-guidance).

### Laboratory methods

All samples were analysed by our accredited laboratory at Millbrook. Methods were unchanged from 2024, except that PFAS screening was added in July.

## Results

| Parameter | Limit | Median | Maximum | Compliant |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| E. coli (per 100 ml) | 0 | 0 | 0 | 100% |
| Nitrate (mg/l) | 50 | 18.2 | 44.9 | 100% |
| Lead (µg/l) | 10 | 0.6 | 14.1 | 99.1% |
| Turbidity (NTU) | 4 | 0.08 | 1.2 | 100% |

### Microbiological

No E. coli was found in any sample leaving a treatment works. Coliform bacteria were detected in 3 of 2,760 reservoir samples; each was resampled clear within 48 hours.

### Chemical

Nitrate at Eastmoor averaged 31 mg/l, against 27 mg/l in 2023. Blending with the Hollins source keeps supply well within the limit.

## Incidents

We reported four incidents to the regulator:

1. A turbidity rise at Hollins after storm Bert (February).
2. A burst main at Crane Street affecting 1,900 homes (May).
3. A chlorine dosing fault at Millbrook (August).
4. A taste and odour complaint cluster in Westfield (November).

## Outlook

For 2026 we will extend PFAS screening to all works and start a lead pipe replacement grant scheme with the city council.
